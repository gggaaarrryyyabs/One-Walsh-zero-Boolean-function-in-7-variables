The switching method in $7$ and $8$ variables, yields $13112220$ and $2^{67}+3·2^{62}$ EA-inequivalent classes in 7 and 8 variables, respectively, using the 97 seven-variable and 775 eight-variable classes provided in [1]. 
However, those functions are all have algebraic degree 6 and 7, respectively, as our swithcing method is only suitable for n-1.
The file in this repo is the result, verified by computer, of applying switching method to the first function **336666333ca5c3a5f9202c2079a0aca0** appeared in [https://github.com/MJauberty/On-the-Cardinality-of-the-Walsh-Support](https://github.com/MJauberty/On-the-Cardinality-of-the-Walsh-Support/blob/main/cardinality_127_functions.txt), see [1].
It generates 4096 distinct EA classes, all have one Walsh zero and have algebraic degree 6.
The number $2^{67}+3·2^{62}$ are an estimation using the Burside's lemma, when applying to 22 eight-variable one-Walsh-zero functions in [1], which are selected due to the large cardinality of support of $h$.
Where $c_f=\sum_x x\ne 0$, $\mathbb{F}_2^n={0,c_f}\oplus V$ and $f$ in ${0,c_f}\oplus V$ is in the form of $f(t,u)=g(u)+h(u)t$. 


[1] Maxence Jauberty and Pierrick M´eaux. On the cardinality of the Walsh support of a Boolean function. Cryptography and Communications, pages 1–54, 2026.
